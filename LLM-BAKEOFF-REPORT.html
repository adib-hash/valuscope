Findings

  • This isn't judge favouritism. Gemini 3.1 Pro, judging blind, ranked all four Claude models above all four Gemini models on summaries. Its scores for the Claude models (Opus 9.2, Fable 8.8, Sonnet 8.7, Haiku 7.8) were close to, and slightly above, the Claude judge's.
  • Gemini's summaries are accurate but thin. Gemini 3.1 Pro had one of the best accuracy scores (9.1) and the lowest coverage (5.1). The Gemini models write about 430 to 580 words and the Claude models 750 to 1,100. The judges rewarded catching guidance changes and analyst pushback. If length on a phone worries you, Sonnet has the best concision of the top three (8.1).
  • No model made up numbers. The figure check found every number in all 48 summaries in its transcript except one, and that one was arithmetic: Gemini 3.8 Flash added Kinsale's 24 wholesale and 176 retail broker appointments into "200". The judges' errors were subtler. The commonest were reversed causality (Gemini 3.6 Flash said JPMorgan named co-presidents because Marianne Lake was retiring; the transcript says the reverse), hedges turned into expectations, and figures put in the wrong period.
  • A strict-grounding edge case. Kinsale's CFO said net income rose 31.1% "quarter-over-quarter". Opus, Sonnet and Fable wrote "year over year", which is almost certainly what he meant, and the Gemini judge marked it as an error. That's the right call under ValuScope's "transcript only" rule, and also a sign the Claude models sometimes fix the transcript.
  • Sonnet 5.5 broke on comp picks. At low effort it returned two peers for Celsius plus Celsius itself, and for Hims it returned three peers and a literal "HIMS_PLACEHOLDER". It's the best value for summaries and an unreliable comp picker, so the two features shouldn't share a model just because one is good.
  • Every model suggests dead tickers. Between 6% and 11% of suggestions were dead or recycled symbols: SQ (Block is now XYZ), CYBR (bought by Palo Alto), UDMY, FI and OLO. Only Claude Opus 5.5 knew Block's new ticker. The live-quote check in peers.js is essential; keep it whatever model runs.
  • Two concrete production bugs. CrowdStrike's curated set is MSFT, AMZN, GOOGL, SNOW, MDB, DDOG, NET and NVDA. getCuratedComps merges CRWD's three groups in table order and cuts off at eight before it reaches the Cybersecurity group. Toast's similar-stocks rung returns IOT, GTLB, MDB, DOCN, RBRK and MNDY. They share Yahoo's "Software - Infrastructure" label but have nothing to do with restaurant payments. Cava's set is padded with On Running.
  • Gemini 3.1 Pro rates its own comp sets highly. Its judge gave its own sets 9.0, while Claude's judge gave them 6.8. Even on Claude's judge alone it ties Opus for first, so the pick stands, but the real margin is smaller than the overall column suggests.
  • Method note. The Anthropic key ran out of credit partway through judging. Claude's judge scored all six summaries and two comp tickers by API; the other ten comp tickers were scored by the same model (Opus 5.5) running as a Claude Code subagent. It used the same blinded prompt and saw nothing else, the method the Kitab bake-off also used. Each model ran once per input, so per-ticker scores are noisy and the averages are what count. The prompts and schemas are the deployed ones. Gemini 3.6 Flash ran at low thinking, as in the uncommitted gemini.js change in the working tree; the deployed version still uses the default (higher) thinking level, which that change found produced the same output more slowly.
